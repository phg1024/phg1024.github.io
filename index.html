  • Teaching an Old Ender 3 New Speeds

    My Ender 3 V2 took almost three hours for a part the new printer makes in eighty minutes. One evening I moved it from its stock firmware to Klipper: a fresh OctoPi first, firmware built in Docker on the Mac, PID and extrusion checks, and an accelerometer on the Pi to measure how the frame shakes. The printer can now accelerate almost six times as hard.

  • Dictation That Stays on My Mac

    Hold a key, talk, let go, and the words appear wherever the cursor is. My first version, a Python app Gemini wrote, didn't quite work: its menu bar icon never appeared, and any web page could read what I'd dictated. So it became a native Mac app, with a small speech model that transcribes five seconds of speech in 50 ms and never touches the network. It's now free on GitHub.

  • The FPGA Finally Earns Its Keep

    My home-built KVM ran at 15 fps because two small ARM cores were encoding JPEG in software. So I wrote a JPEG encoder in C++ for the FPGA sitting right next to them. Now it streams 1080p at the full 60 fps of its HDMI input, after a fight with timing, a full chip, a crashing toolchain and, at the very end, one sleep() in Python.

  • A KVM From the Spare-Board Drawer

    My home server has no monitor and no keyboard, and the one time it needed both, I was on the floor beside it. So I turned an FPGA board with an HDMI input and a Pi Zero into a KVM I can open in a browser. What the FPGA did, what it didn't, and the DMA that kept writing after its program died.

  • You Get What You Pay For, Plus Extra Yellow

    Genuine toner cost five times as much, so I bought third-party, and every print came out yellow. I put a color-correcting print queue in front of the printer and calibrated it with the printer's own scanner, until the printer moved under me at midnight. What fixed it in the end, and why a picture book outranked the scanner.
